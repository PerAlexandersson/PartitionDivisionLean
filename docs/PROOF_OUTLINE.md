# Lean proof outline for partition division

## Target and status

Let `k ≥ 1`. On the monomial basis of symmetric functions, define
`R_k(m_λ) = m_(λ/k)` if every part of `λ` is divisible by `k`, and zero
otherwise. For `μ ⊢ kn`, the theorem to formalize is

```text
R_k(e_μ) = ∑_{ν ⊢ n} c_{μ,ν} e_ν,  with c_{μ,ν} ∈ ℕ.
```

The companion manuscript proves this theorem mathematically. We have not
formalized any part of it here. The route below follows its finite free-basis,
Pieri, and trace argument. A finite graph route is listed separately because
it proves useful special cases but not the full theorem term by term.

Module layout and reuse of existing Lean libraries are in
[`ARCHITECTURE.md`](ARCHITECTURE.md); the work is tracked in issue #15.

## Dependency order

```text
finite exponent filter ────────────────┐
restricted partitions → free basis ────┼→ trace/filter identity ──┐
Macdonald factorization → Pieri limit ─┴→ positive matrices ─────┼→ rational
positive-matrix trace kernel ─────────────────────────────────────┘  positivity
integral monomial and e-bases ─────────────────────────────────────→ integrality
```

The positive-matrix kernel is independent of Macdonald polynomials and can
be verified first. It must not be named or presented as the final theorem.

## 1. Finite operator and stable bridge

In `N` variables, filter monomials whose **individual** exponents are
divisible by `k`. For `f` in the symmetric subring, prove the exact identity

```text
filter_k(f)(z₁,...,z_N) = (R_k f)(z₁^k,...,z_N^k).
```

Start with monomials, extend linearly, and prove symmetry is preserved.
Define `e_μ` using Mathlib's finite elementary symmetric polynomials. Show
that finite specialization detects an output of degree `n` when `N ≥ n`.
Keep the integral monomial and elementary lattices explicit: `R_k` is
integral on the first, and the second is an integral basis. These facts turn
the final rational nonnegativity into nonnegative **integer** coefficients.

Suggested modules: `Finite/ExponentFilter.lean`, `Finite/Elementary.lean`,
`Finite/Stability.lean`. The precise declaration names should follow the
Mathlib API actually found, not be fixed by this note.

## 2. Unconditional positive-trace kernel

Represent polynomial entries with nonnegative rational coefficients using
`MvPolynomial σ NNRat`; map them to rational coefficients only at the boundary.
Prove that finite matrix products and their traces retain coefficientwise
nonnegativity. The kernel should cover any finite list of transition matrices
and empty products. This is an independent lemma, not a hypothesis standing
in for the Macdonald construction.

Mathlib already has matrix trace, trace base change, multivariate polynomial
coefficient maps, and nonnegative rationals. Audit exact signatures before
implementing `PositiveTrace.lean`.

## 3. Restricted states and the finite free basis

For a partition with at most `N` parts, divide adjacent differences by `k`.
Prove the unique decomposition into a `k`-restricted partition and a
partition multiplied by `k`. The restricted states number `k^N`.

The manuscript uses specialized Macdonald `P` functions indexed by these
states as a basis of the finite symmetric-polynomial algebra over the
subalgebra generated by `e_i(x₁^k,...,x_N^k)`. The proof requires:

1. regularity of this specialization;
2. duality and root-of-unity factorization in the manuscript normalization;
3. the resulting factorization into restricted states and elementary
   generators; and
4. freeness, not merely spanning, of the `k^N` states.

Mathlib does not currently provide the needed Macdonald `P`, `Q'`, or Pieri
theory. This is the largest missing formalization layer. Do not introduce
its conclusions as axioms or silently replace them by a conditional theorem.

The Descouens–Morita factorization deserves an explicit subgoal. Its
published proof does not account for Pieri outputs that lose the added
rectangular block. The fuller manuscript supplies a proposed zero-coefficient
repair. Prove both the characterization of those outputs and the vanishing
numerator with nonzero denominators before marking this dependency closed.

## 4. Positive Pieri transition matrices

Formalize only the partition-cell and hook data needed by the elementary
Pieri rule. For a `k`-restricted input, a cell in the relevant unchanged-row,
changed-column set with zero input leg has hook length strictly between `0`
and `k`; hence it is not divisible by `k`. For divisible hooks with positive
leg `l`, the local specialization has value `(l+1)/l > 0`.

After specializing `q` to a primitive `k`th root and `t` along `qv`, evaluate
at `v=1` only after proving each entry regular. The resulting finite matrices
must have entries in nonnegative rational polynomials in the independent
elementary generators. Prove this for each Pieri transition, including states
whose output reduces to a different restricted state.

## 5. Trace equals the exponent filter

For the finite free extension in §3, prove that the trace of multiplication
by `f` equals `k^N` times the coordinatewise exponent filter. One route is
to base-change to a field containing independent `k`th roots, split into the
`k^N` coordinatewise root twists, and diagonalize multiplication. The root
sum kills any monomial with an exponent not divisible by `k`.

The proof needs an injective base-change map, a product-algebra splitting,
and compatibility of trace with base change. The factor `k^N` is essential.
Keep the elementary root-average identity as an earlier standalone lemma.

## 6. End-to-end theorem

The trace of multiplication by `e_μ` is the trace of the product of its
Pieri transition matrices. By §2 and §4, its coefficients in the independent
`e_i(x^k)` generators are nonnegative rationals. By §5, the same trace is
`k^N R_k(e_μ)(x^k)`. For `N ≥ n`, coefficient comparison and §1 give
nonnegative rational `e`-coefficients of `R_k(e_μ)`; the integral-lattice
argument upgrades them to nonnegative integers.

The public final Lean declaration should have no Macdonald factorization,
positive-matrix existence, or trace identity among its hypotheses. Such
conditional statements are useful intermediate lemmas, but not the target.
